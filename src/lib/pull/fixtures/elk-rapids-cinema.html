Coyote vs. Acme (2026) is a live-action/animated legal comedy that reimagines the classic Looney Tunes rivalry by asking what happens when Wile E. Coyote finally decides to fight back against the Acme Corporation. More About the Movie
Tony (2026) is a biographical drama that follows the young Anthony Bourdain during the pivotal summer that would set him on the path toward becoming one of America's most celebrated chefs, writers, and television personalities. Set in 1975, the film follows 19-year-old Tony as he travels to Provincetown, Massachusetts, where a chance job at a chaotic seafood restaurant introduces him to the demanding world of professional cooking.

Starring Dominic Sessa as Anthony Bourdain and Antonio Banderas as the restaurant's head chef, alongside Emilia Jones, Dagmara Domińczyk, Rich Sommer, Stavros Halkias, and Leo Woodall, Tony explores the friendships, mistakes, mentorship, and youthful ambition that shaped Bourdain's early life. From unruly kitchen camaraderie and personal struggles to a disastrous grease fire that threatens the restaurant, the film offers a fictionalized portrait of the formative experiences that ultimately inspired a remarkable thirty-year culinary career.

More About the Movie
The Extraordinary Caterpillar is a visually stunning documentary that invites audiences into the remarkable hidden world of one of nature's most important—and often overlooked—creatures. Through breathtaking 4K macro cinematography, the film reveals the extraordinary diversity, beauty, and ecological importance of caterpillars, showing how these tiny animals form the foundation of healthy forests, gardens, and wildlife habitats.

Featuring the work of renowned naturalists and scientists, including Sam Jaffe of The Caterpillar Lab, entomologist Dr. David Wagner, and ecologist Dr. Doug Tallamy, the documentary explores the vital role caterpillars play in sustaining birds, pollinators, and countless other species. Both educational and uplifting, The Extraordinary Caterpillar reminds us that some of nature's greatest wonders—and greatest opportunities for conservation—exist just outside our own front doors.
